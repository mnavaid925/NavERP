A run writes fraud alerts and nothing else. It holds no purchase order, blocks no payment, refuses no award, raises no vendor block and never edits, merges or deactivates a supplier record. Park, do not block: the rules produce questions, and people answer them.

What each rule looks for

deterministic comparisons, stated in full
Vendor and employee share an identity attribute
A supplier and an employee with the same tax id, the same address, or the same phone number or mailbox. Flags the overlap and names both sides; a family business the company knows about looks identical from the data.
Requisition approved by its own requester
The approver on a signature is the same user who raised the requisition. The one rule here with no judgement in it at all — either the two ids match or they do not.
Duplicate / shell supplier record
Two supplier records sharing a normalised name, a tax id or an address. FLAGGED ONLY — nothing merges, deactivates or edits either record.
Purchase order raised after the invoice it authorises
An order dated later than the invoice it is supposed to cover, past the grace period. Different from an invoice with no order at all, which belongs to spend analytics.
New spend against an unresolved screening match
An order committed to a supplier whose sanctions screening, dated on or before that order, still carries a match nobody adjudicated. A hit found afterwards does not count: that would be hindsight.
New supplier with immediate high-value spend
A supplier invoiced past the threshold inside its first thirty days. One alert per supplier, pointing at the largest single invoice in the run.

What an alert is, and what it is not

rules, not accusations

Each row records something a deterministic rule found true about records this workspace already holds: a supplier and an employee sharing a tax id, an address or a mailbox; a requisition signed off by the person who raised it; two supplier records that look like one company; an order dated after the invoice it authorises; spend committed against a supplier whose sanctions match nobody resolved; a brand-new supplier taking high-value spend at once.

Every one of those has an innocent explanation as well as a guilty one. A shared address can be a family business the company already knows about. A duplicate supplier record is usually a data-entry mistake. That is why an alert is parked, never enforced: raising one holds no purchase order, blocks no payment, refuses no award and never edits, merges or deactivates a supplier record. The only place a vendor is actually blocked is the vendor suspension register, and that stays a separate, deliberate decision.

A survey KPI has nothing to average until people are asked. Request a handful of responses for a period — a buyer, a quality engineer, somebody in AP — and the importance-weighted mean of the submitted ones becomes that KPI’s measured value. Ask the supplier to rate itself on the same scale and the gap between the two answers is the conversation worth having.

Ratings become 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 for the survey aggregate, weighted by each respondent’s importance (0–10). A response filed at importance 0 still counts as a respondent and moves the number by nothing — so a page can honestly say eight people answered while reporting that six of them shaped the score. Only submitted, internal responses feed a KPI; a supplier’s self-assessment is read next to our score, never folded into it.

Which receipt, and who gets paid

Only received goods receipts are offered. A receipt that has not been received has posted nothing to the stock ledger yet, so there is no stock for the cost to land on — a voucher built against one would have to refuse to allocate, which is a worse answer than not letting it be created. A cancelled receipt is refused outright.
The payee is the vendor this voucher will be billed to, usually the freight forwarder or the customs broker. It is required, because a vendor bill without a vendor cannot exist in Accounts Payable. Individual charge lines may name a different vendor — those are still spread onto stock but are left off the drafted bill and counted back to you, because one bill goes to one vendor.

The trade context

Optional — every field here can be left blank
{% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.shipment %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.trade_document %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.currency %}
The shipment is how several receipts that travelled together are tied back to one voyage or container, without inventing a voyage table nobody would maintain. The trade document is where the declared customs value and the Incoterm come from — they are read off it rather than retyped here, so correcting the paperwork corrects the voucher instead of leaving two versions of the same fact.
Currency is a shared master across the whole system, not a per-workspace list, so the choices here are every active currency rather than only ones this workspace has used before. Leaving it blank means the voucher carries no explicit currency and the drafted bill inherits whatever Accounts Payable defaults to.

Cost date, and how the charges spread

The basis can be overridden per charge line
{% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.cost_date %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.allocation_basis %}
By value is the usual answer and the default: an expensive item absorbs more of the freight than a cheap one of the same size, which is what most tax authorities expect to see. By quantity suits a container of identical cartons; by weight suits sea and road freight; by volume suits air freight and anything cubed out; equal is the flat split for a fee that has nothing to do with what was in the box, like a single customs entry filing.
A basis nothing can measure falls back rather than failing: if the items carry no weights, a by-weight charge spreads by quantity, and if the quantities are all zero it splits equally. The basis actually used is stamped on every allocation row, so a fallback is visible on the voucher afterwards rather than being a silent substitution. Weight and volume come from the item master — items with neither contribute nothing to those two bases, which is exactly why the fallback exists.
The cost date is when the charges are recognised, and it is what the variance report and the budget report group on. It is not the receipt date and does not have to match it — freight is routinely invoiced weeks after the goods land.

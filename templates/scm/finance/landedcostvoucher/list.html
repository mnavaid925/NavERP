Landed Cost Vouchers

Supply Chain›Landed Cost Vouchers

Freight, duty, brokerage, insurance and handling do not stop being part of what a thing cost just because they arrive on a different invoice from the goods. A voucher gathers those charges against one goods receipt, spreads them across the stock that receipt actually put on the shelf, and rolls the result into each item's average cost. Estimated and actual sit side by side the whole way through, so the gap between what was quoted and what was billed is a number you can see rather than one you find at year end.

A voucher starts from a received goods receipt: pick the receipt, name the vendor that will be billed, give the cost date, and save. Nothing is spread at that moment — you add the freight, duty, brokerage and handling charges to the voucher afterwards and then press Allocate, which divides them across the stock that receipt actually posted to the ledger and rolls the result into each item's average cost. If you were expecting rows here, try clearing the filters.

Estimated, actual and variance are stored figures the last recalculation left behind — they are not recomputed as you read this page, so a voucher whose charges have changed since shows the last saved number until something recalculates it. Edit and Delete disappear the moment a voucher is allocated, because by then its cost is inside the average cost of real items and a silent edit would leave the two disagreeing with no record of why; cancel the voucher instead, which reverses the roll properly and keeps every line as evidence.

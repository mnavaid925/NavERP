{{ append_only_note }}

Rows below carry a View action and, where the attempt is not already settled, a Retry button. There is no Add, no Edit and no Delete, and none of the three is missing: a delivery record is evidence of what this workspace tried to send, and a row somebody can rewrite after a subscriber has complained is not evidence of anything. A further attempt is appended as a new state on the row’s ladder, never typed in by hand.

Retry stamps the next rung of the backoff schedule onto the row — the published eight-attempt ladder that runs 0s, 5s, 5m, 30m, 2h, 5h, 10h, 10h, roughly thirty-two hours end to end. It sends nothing: 4.19 ships no outbound transport, so no subscriber is called, no payload is signed and no delivery is attempted. It records when the next attempt is due, and nothing runs on a clock to act on that. Pressing it opens the row so you can see the result.

Rows arrive here from the attempt itself and from nowhere else — no screen in this application writes one by hand, and none edits or removes one. If a row looks wrong, that is what the log says happened. Staff changes to the subscriptions themselves are a different trail entirely and live in the audit log, unchanged. Payload excerpts are truncated by design and may contain partner PII, so a full payload body is never stored, here or anywhere in 4.19 — and the signature column on the detail page is blank on every row for a related reason: with no plaintext signing key and no transport, there is nothing that could compute one.

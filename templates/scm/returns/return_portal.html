Nothing waiting

Returns a customer raised through the portal arrive here as requests. Approving one is a decision a person makes on the return itself — a policy may pre-fill the approval form, but nothing in this module approves itself, because every approval records who made it.
Only requests that came in through the portal appear here. A return raised by a customer service agent, by email or by phone is the same record with a different source and lives on the returns list.

No live tracking links

Every return gets an unguessable link the moment it is created, and it resolves while the return is live — a draft or cancelled one gives a customer nothing at all rather than leaking that it exists.
Treat these links as the credential they are. The token is the only thing guarding the page: it never expires, cannot be rotated and cannot be revoked, so a forwarded link is permanent read access to that one return. The status page shows no prices and no costs; the printed slip carries the return-to address, so it grants more than the status page does and the two are not interchangeable.

No active policies

A policy is the promise the shop makes: how long the window is, what the clock runs from, which outcomes may be offered and what a returned unit is worth back in stock. Without one no return can be measured against anything, and every eligibility check falls back to a human's judgement.
The most specific policy wins — a category policy beats a blanket one, and among equals the lowest priority number does. A delivery-based window falls through to the fallback more often than it looks like it should, because the delivery stamp on a sales order is something a human records by hand; the eligibility verdict on each return therefore shows which date the clock actually ran from.

No active reason codes

A reason is policy, not a label: what the customer says went wrong decides who pays the return freight, whether the unit may ever go back into sellable stock, and which outcomes they may even be offered. The reason narrows what the policy allows — it can never widen it.
A customer with a portal account raises their own return through the customer request form, where the customer and the source are forced by the server rather than taken from the submission. Anonymous return requests are deliberately not built: nothing lets a stranger prove they own an order, and there is no postal code stored anywhere to check one against.

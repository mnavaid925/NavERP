Warranty Claims

Supply Chain›Warranty Claims

The supplier-recovery side of a return. A claim is a two-party negotiation — claimed, then approved, then credited — against a named supplier, with their own deadline for answering. Nothing here posts stock and nothing here drafts an accounting document: accounting.Bill has no kind field, so there is no vendor credit note to raise. The amounts are recorded and the recovery rate is computed from them.

No warranty claims

When a returned unit failed because of how it was made rather than how it was used, the money goes back up the chain rather than out of your pocket. Raise a claim against the supplier, list the part, the labour and the freight separately, and record what they actually credit — a partial approval that accepts the part and refuses the labour is the normal outcome.
{% include "partials/pagination.html" %}

Claimed is the sum of the typed cost lines — what the supplier is being asked for. Approved is what they came back with, and credited is what actually arrived; the three are separate columns because they routinely differ, and a recovery rate computed from a single flat figure would be a lie.

Submitting a claim, recording the supplier's response and recording their credit are workspace-administrator actions — they speak to a counterparty and they move the recovery figures. Drafting and editing a claim is ordinary work, and only a draft may be edited or deleted.

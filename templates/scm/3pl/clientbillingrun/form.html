Everything else on the model — status, subtotal, minimum_adjustment, total, calculated_at, approved_at, approved_by and invoice — is editable=False, so a ModelForm cannot reach it and a crafted POST naming one has nowhere to land (L22). That is the mechanism, not a policy: * `status` moves ONLY along the verb ladder on the run's own page, each verb validating the transition it makes and writing its own audit entry. A status field here would let someone type an approved run into existence with nobody's name on the approval. * `subtotal` / `minimum_adjustment` / `total` are written only by recalc_amounts(), summing the lines in Python. A typed total is a total nobody can take apart, which is the exact failure this whole sub-module exists to avoid. * `invoice` is set by Draft Invoice and by nothing else. Pointing a run at an arbitrary invoice would be an IDOR wearing a dropdown. Nothing on this page prices anything. Saving creates (or re-dates) an EMPTY run; the figures appear when you press Calculate on the run itself. That separation is deliberate — a period you can correct before pricing is cheaper than an invoice you have to credit afterwards. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- WHAT THE FORM REFUSES, AND WHERE -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Every rule described in the help text below is enforced by ClientBillingRun.clean() and never here: an end before the start, a period longer than the year cap, a rate card belonging to a DIFFERENT client, and a rate card not in force for any part of the period. The client-match rule is the one that matters most — billing one client at another client's rates is the single mistake in this sub-module that produces a plausible-looking invoice which is entirely wrong — and the model enforces it even though the dropdown is narrowed, because a narrowed dropdown has never held against a crafted POST.

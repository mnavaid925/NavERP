How this board is worked out

Nothing on this page is stored. Each active plan is asked for its own due status, and the answer is whichever axis arrives first: a calendar plan compares next_due_on against today, a meter plan compares next_due_reading against the asset's latest reading, a condition plan compares that reading against its threshold, and a combined plan takes the earlier of the calendar and the meter. That is why a plan with a date still in the future can sit in Overdue — the machine has already passed the usage target — and the row's reason says exactly that.

Generate is the only action here that creates anything, and it is one deliberate press: it snapshots the plan's checklist onto a new work order as plain copies (so editing the plan next month never rewrites what a technician was asked to check), stamps the captured due date as the job's scheduled start, and advances the schedule by exactly one cycle even if several were missed — erasing the gap would erase the fact that cycles were missed. It refuses outright for an inactive plan or one that already has an outstanding job, because a duplicate splits one service's checklist, parts and cost across two documents that nothing afterwards can pair back up.

This page moves no stock and posts no journal entry. Parts leave the storeroom when a job issues them, which writes a negative movement into 4.3's ledger; the money belongs to Accounting.

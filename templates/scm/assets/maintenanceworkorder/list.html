Maintenance Work Orders

Supply Chain›Maintenance Work Orders

A maintenance request, a generated preventive occurrence and a breakdown are the same document at different points on one ladder — so they live in one register rather than in a notification table joined to an order table. That is what makes "what has this machine cost us, and how often does it stop?" a single group by instead of a union over two grains. This is not the production work order: that one makes product against a BOM, this one repairs a machine and consumes spares against an asset.

No maintenance work orders

Nothing has been raised against a machine yet. Raise one here and it starts as a request; approving it, scheduling it and starting it are buttons on its own page, not a status you type. A preventive plan generates one of these on its due date, so the planned and the reactive job end up in the same register with the same costs and the same downtime. Ordered by reported at, newest first. The cost column is issued parts + labour hours × labour rate + the contractor's charge; a part that has only been planned contributes nothing, because its unit cost is stamped from the item's average cost at the instant it is issued and never read live afterwards — average cost moves with every receipt, and a live read would quietly restate the cost of a repair closed last quarter every time a new delivery landed. Issuing parts is the only thing in this sub-module that writes the stock ledger, and it posts no journal entry: the contractor's charge is recorded here, not billed from here.

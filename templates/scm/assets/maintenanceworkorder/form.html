The machine and the people

The plan list offers every plan in the workspace rather than only the chosen asset's: on a new job no asset has been picked yet, and a dropdown narrowed to "plans for this asset" would render permanently empty on the screen that matters. A plan that maintains a different machine is refused on save instead — which also holds against a crafted request, as a narrowed dropdown never did. Reported by and assigned to offer people on the payroll; the contractor takes the commercial party list. The parts storeroom is where spares are drawn from, and without it the Issue Parts button on the job's page is refused outright — there is no sensible default, because guessing a storeroom would draw stock out of a bin that never held it.

When

Reported at is editable because an operator routinely logs a fault that happened on the night shift. The outage

These two are editable, unlike the start and completion stamps, and that is deliberate: discovering afterwards that the machine actually went down at 02:10 is the normal case, and a window that could only be set by a button could never be corrected. The end may not precede the start, and the total downtime is derived from the pair on save — it is never typed, because typing it would be typing a subtraction that could then disagree with its own two inputs. Tick unplanned for a genuine failure: that is the figure availability and mean time between failures are actually about, and counting a planned shutdown as one would make every well-maintained machine look unreliable.

Failure chain

Problem → cause → remedy

Three closed vocabularies — the symptom observed, why it happened, and what was done about it. A closed list is the entire reason "which cause costs us the most downtime this year?" is a single grouped query rather than a text-mining project, which is why free text is not offered here. Each list carries an honest Other, because a technician forced into a wrong code makes all the other codes untrustworthy. All three are optional: the cause and the remedy are usually only knowable once the job is done.

What it costs

Labour is hours × the charge-out rate, and the rate is capped rather than clamped — an absurd figure does not stay local, it flows into this job's total, into the asset's maintenance cost to date and into the repair-versus-replace ratio on the depreciation report, so it is refused on the way in rather than quietly trimmed on the way out. The contractor's charge is recorded here and nowhere else: Asset Management drafts no accounting document and posts no journal entry. The parts cost is not on this form at all — it comes from the lines below, and only once they have actually been issued from stock.

Meter capture

A capture field, not a stored meter. When the job completes, a value here becomes a reading filed against the asset with this job as its reference — the reading log stays the single place a meter is ever read from, and this column is never read back. If the asset names no meter the capture cannot be filed and the Complete button says so out loud rather than saving it under an invented name.

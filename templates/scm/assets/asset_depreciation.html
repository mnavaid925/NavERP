Supply Chain› Assets› Depreciation

What each machine is still worth against what it has cost to keep running. Book value is read from Accounting; maintenance spend is the SCM side. The ratio between them is arithmetic — spend ÷ book value — and the division is printed on every row so nothing here has to be taken on trust. This is not a model and not a prediction.

How to read this

Repair vs replace = maintenance spend ÷ book value × 100. Maintenance spend is the SCM side and is real money already committed: issued parts at the cost they were actually drawn at, plus labour hours × rate, plus contractor charges, over every job on that machine that was not cancelled. A cancelled job may still carry a labour figure typed before it was called off, and counting it would charge the asset for work nobody did.

The two thresholds are a rule of thumb, not a policy engine: past {{ watch_pct }}% the cumulative repairs are worth more than half what the machine is still worth, which is the conventional point to start planning a replacement; past {{ replace_pct }}% it has cost more to keep than it is worth. Neither is a decision — criticality, lead time, spares availability and what a replacement would actually cost all sit outside this page.

The statutory figures and their GL postings belong to Accounting. Acquisition cost, salvage value, useful life, method and accumulated depreciation are all maintained on the fixed asset, and the depreciation journal is posted there. 4.13 stores none of them, recomputes none of them, and writes no journal entry from this screen — because two writers of one accumulated figure is precisely how the two numbers on the two pages come to disagree.

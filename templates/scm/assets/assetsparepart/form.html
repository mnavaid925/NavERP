There is no separate spare-parts master and there never will be: the item is the same one the storeroom counts, with its own unit of measure, category, costing method and on-hand across every location. A parallel parts table would duplicate all of it and then need reconciling with the ledger a maintenance issue posts into. The list is not narrowed to items flagged as spares, on purpose — a machine legitimately consumes ordinary consumables like grease and filters that nobody has flagged, and narrowing would make the obvious line un-addable with no way to see why. Each item may appear once per asset; a duplicate is refused as a field error rather than accepted and left to collide.

How it matters

Mark a line critical when running out of it stops the job — not merely when the part is expensive. That flag is what separates "the service is delayed" from "the technician is standing at the machine with nothing to fit", and it is the one thing on this form that changes how a shortage reads. The quantity per service is what one service consumes, held to four decimal places, and it is a planning figure only: it is never posted anywhere and never draws stock down by itself.

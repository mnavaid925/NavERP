{% if obj.trigger_type == "condition" %} A condition plan has no cycle to roll: its next occurrence is decided by the machine, not by a schedule, so generating a job leaves the trigger exactly where it is and the plan comes due again the moment a reading says so. There is deliberately no equality operator — a continuous reading almost never lands exactly on a threshold, so an “equals” trigger would look configured and never fire. {% elif obj.trigger_type == "combined" %} The two axes are evaluated together, and the usage axis is consulted first: a machine that has already run past its target outranks a date still in the future. A combined plan reading Overdue on a future date is not a bug — the work was owed at the meter and the calendar simply has not caught up. {% elif obj.schedule_basis == "floating" %} Floating measures the next occurrence from what actually happened, so a service done a fortnight late pushes the whole future schedule a fortnight out. That is the right reading for wear-driven work. {% else %} Fixed measures from the calendar the plan already published, so a job done late does not drag every future cycle late with it. That is the right reading for a statutory inspection due in January whatever happened in December. {% endif %}

This plan has no steps

It can still raise a job — the work order simply carries no checklist, and the technician gets the prose instructions and nothing to tick. Adding steps here is what gives every cycle the same instructions and gives an auditor something to read afterwards. Edit the plan to add them.
Generating a job copies these steps as plain columns, with no link back. That is deliberate: editing this plan next month must not rewrite what a job three weeks ago actually asked a technician to check and sign against. A step marked mandatory is one the job cannot be signed off without; optional steps exist so nobody is forced to tick something that did not apply, which is what keeps the ticks on the mandatory ones worth reading. Safety steps — isolation, lock-out/tag-out, permit — are flagged rather than kept in a separate permit register, so they stay visible and printable on the job itself.

The plan stays editable at any time, including once it has raised jobs — a standing instruction is re-scoped, re-timed and re-crewed, and a screen that froze the row the moment it generated something would push those corrections into a notes field. Nothing an edit does reaches a job that already exists. Deleting is the honest opposite: the checklist above is cascaded away and cannot be rebuilt, while every job survives with its own snapshot. If the schedule is simply finished with, untick Active on the edit form — an inactive plan never comes due, cannot generate, and keeps its whole history.

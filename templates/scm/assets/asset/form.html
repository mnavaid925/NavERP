What you type here is the asset as the plant knows it — what it is, where it sits, who looks after it, what it cost and which meter it runs on. Nothing the module derives is on this form: reliability, availability, downtime and maintenance cost are recomputed from the jobs and readings that produce them, so there is no figure here for anyone to disagree with. An asset stays editable at any status, deliberately — a retired machine is often the row that most needs correcting.

Make, model and markings

The tag is the QR or barcode value physically on the asset, and it must resolve to exactly one machine: a save that would put the same tag on a second asset is refused and the message names the one already carrying it. Leave it blank on anything untagged — a blank tag is not a clash, and any number of assets may be untagged. Specifications is free text on purpose: capacity, voltage, rated output, ratings. A real user-defined-field engine belongs to Module 11, and inventing half of one here would be a schema everybody then has to migrate off.

Where it sits, who looks after it

The parent makes this a component of a larger assembly. The asset itself is never offered as its own parent, and a longer loop — A under B under A — is refused on save with the row that would close it named, because an ancestry walk through a cycle never finishes. The work centre is the link that lets a repair explain a capacity hole: taking a machine down is not only a maintenance fact, it is a production one, and the load board reads the centre. Custodian is narrowed to people, supplier and service vendor to commercial counterparties — but a narrowed dropdown is a courtesy, and every one of these is re-checked against this workspace on save.

Commercial

Commissioned on is where the reliability clock starts: availability and MTBF are measured against the window that opens on that date, so an asset without one is measured from the day its record was created instead — which is honest but usually flatters it. The warranty date drives a derived chip and the renewal buckets on the register; leave it blank and the asset reads "Not recorded", which is muted and never green, because not knowing whether something is under warranty is not the same as knowing it is. The capitalised record is a pointer at Accounting and nothing more — 4.13 stores no cost basis, no useful life and no accumulated depreciation, posts no journal entry, and only ever reads the book value it shows.

The meter

This declares the meter; it does not record a value. There is no "current reading" column anywhere in the module — readings live in their own append-only log, in exactly the spirit of the stock ledger, because a single mutable number cannot answer a meter-based service interval, a usage trend or a condition trigger, and it would have two writers the moment a technician can type one on a work order as well. Name the primary meter and a plan measured in those units will find its readings; a reading logged under a different name is deliberately never substituted, since an odometer figure handed to a running-hours interval would schedule a service roughly never and fail silently.

Housekeeping

Clearing Active takes the asset out of the live-plant filters without touching a single job, reading or plan hanging off it. That is the move to reach for instead of deleting: a delete is refused outright the moment any work order references the asset, and where it is allowed it takes the PM programme, the parts list and the whole meter history with it.

Supply Chain›KPI Targets

The one table in Analytics a human writes. Every other figure in this module is computed live out of procurement, inventory, warehouse, order, transport and returns rows — but nothing in those rows says “six turns is the goal”, “dead means ninety days without a movement”, or “tell me when this slips and tell her”. That intent lives here, and it is what turns a measurement into a target, a tile and an alert.

Ordered by tile order, then name — the same order the pinned targets appear in on the control tower, so what you re-order here is what moves there. The metric list is closed: each key is a promise that a reviewed calculation exists for it, which is what lets a target be validated against the metric's own definition — its legal scopes, which way “good” runs, the parameters it needs — rather than against whatever was typed. Authoring your own formula is Module 10's job, not this one's. The live figure and its band are on each target's own page, not in this table: banding a row means running its whole calculation, and doing that once per row would turn one page of fifteen into fifteen sets of aggregates. Nothing on this screen writes a stock movement or a journal entry — Analytics only ever reads what the rest of the supply chain recorded.

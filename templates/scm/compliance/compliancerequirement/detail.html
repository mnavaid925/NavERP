It POSTs to compliancerequirement_record_check, which is NOT admin-gated: recording that you did the thing you were supposed to do is the daily work of whoever owns the obligation, and putting it behind a workspace administrator is how a register stops being kept at all. Fields are rendered BY NAME, which couples this block to ComplianceCheckForm's whitelist — result / due_date / performed_on / finding / corrective_reference / evidence / notes, all seven of it. `requirement` and `performed_by` are deliberately NOT on the form: the parent comes from the URL, and "who did it" is stamped from request.user, because an audit fact is not an input. The non_field_errors loop is the house shape and is normally inert here: on an invalid POST the view flashes the errors as a message and REDIRECTS, so this form arrives unbound. An obligation stays editable at every status, retired included — duties get re-scoped, re-owned and re-cited, and the row worth editing most is often the one somebody got wrong. Editing never rewrites the proof history: each check snapshots the cycle it answered, so re-scheduling this requirement leaves what a past cycle was due exactly as it was. A row that turns out not to bind you is best kept with its reason rather than deleted — “we considered this and it does not apply, here is why” is itself an auditable answer, and deleting it leaves no answer at all.

Who owns it, and how often

A monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual or biennial obligation needs a first due date or it can never appear on the due queue — it would sit invisible until an auditor found it — so the save is refused without one. One-time and on event have no next cycle and need no date: a pass simply clears it rather than inventing one. The notice window is the staged 90/60/30 reminder pattern permit and certificate-of-insurance trackers use — it is what puts a row in the “due soon” bucket. Deactivated logins are dropped from the owner list, because making somebody who has left answerable is how an obligation ends up with nobody watching it; an owner already stored stays selectable so an unrelated edit cannot silently null them.

Where it comes from

Link-outs, never a second copy of the source record: the agreement already lives in Supplier Contracts and the authorisation in Trade Licences, so an obligation that comes out of one simply points at it. Neither list is filtered to “active”, on purpose — an obligation routinely outlives the paper that created it: a data-retention clause of an expired contract still binds, and a revoked licence's closing conditions still need proving. Deleting either one leaves this requirement and its proof history standing; the evidence that we were compliant outlives the paper. The document is the standing policy, certificate or clause extract the duty rests on.

Where it stands

Status is a human decision here and stays one: opening the register rolls applicable, in progress and overdue forward from the due date, but never touches compliant, non-compliant, not-applicable or retired — a clock must not walk back a judgement. Choosing Not Applicable requires the reason: an unexplained assertion is not auditable, and the row is kept for the reason rather than for the row. Recording a check is done on the requirement's own page, not here.

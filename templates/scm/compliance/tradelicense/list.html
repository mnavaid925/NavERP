Trade Licences & Permits

Supply Chain›Trade Licences

A licence is not paperwork, it is a quantity: authorised for so much value or so many units, and drawn down by every document issued under it. Granted licences, claimed exceptions and general authorisations all live in this one register, because the question asked before a shipment leaves — what authority covers this movement — has an answer nobody trusts when it lives in two tables.

No trade licences

Nothing authorises movement yet. Register an export licence, an ITAR DSP, an EAR/BIS authorisation, an import permit, a customs or hazmat authorisation — or a licence exception, which is claimed rather than granted and belongs in the same register. Give it a ceiling and every document issued under it draws the balance down. If you were expecting rows here, try clearing the filters.
{% include "partials/pagination.html" %}
Ordered by expiry date, soonest first — the order the renewal queue is read in. The used figure is a stored counter with a stated invalidation path: every writer of a charging document re-derives it, and each licence's own page carries a Recompute button, so a wrong balance can never become permanent. Only issued, submitted and accepted documents count against it — a draft or a voided one spends nothing, which is why a licence's page can show more documents than its balance reflects. Nothing on this screen posts a stock movement or drafts an accounting document: a licence authorises movement, the movement itself belongs to Transportation and the money to Accounting.

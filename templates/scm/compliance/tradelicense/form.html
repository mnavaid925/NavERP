What you type here is the authorisation as the authority granted it. The licence's status and its consumed balance are not on this form: the first moves only through the submit, approve and revoke actions, and the second is re-derived from the documents issued under the licence. A licence stays editable at any status precisely because authorities amend, extend and re-scope what they granted — but an edit can only correct what the licence says, never what it has authorised.

Who it covers

Leave the holder blank when the licence is ours; name a party only when it is held by a subsidiary or an agent. The named end user is the final recipient the authority approved — recording it against the licence rather than against a shipment is what makes it checkable before any document is raised. Both dropdowns are narrowed to this workspace's commercial counterparties, and both are re-checked against the workspace on save: a narrowed dropdown is a courtesy, never the guard.

Dates and renewal

The three dates form a ladder — applied for → issued → expires — and the save is refused if they run backwards, including the case where the issue date is blank and the licence would still expire before it was applied for. All three may be left empty; a licence with no expiry never lapses and never enters the renewal queue. The notice window is how far ahead this licence starts reading as "expiring": the status roll uses this number, not a global one, so a permit that takes six months to renew can say so.

The ceilings

This is what makes a licence a quantity rather than a piece of paper. A blank ceiling means unlimited in that dimension — not zero: the licence page prints "Unlimited" and its remaining figure and utilisation read as em dashes rather than as a confident 0 that looks exhausted. A ceiling of 0 is a real and quite different statement, and it authorises nothing. Set either, both or neither. Every document issued under the licence draws the balance down, and an issue that would exceed a ceiling is refused outright rather than clamped to the headroom — a shipment that went out partly unauthorised while the register still looked healthy is exactly the failure a balance exists to prevent.

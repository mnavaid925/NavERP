When it was worked

Date and time, not just a date

The work date must be the calendar day the shift started on — an overnight shift keeps its start date, which is the grain HR attendance uses and the grain a supervisor rosters by. It is pinned to the local date of the clock-in and the save is refused if the two disagree, because a session dated last March holding this week's clock times would poison every period aggregate, every scorecard window and the payroll export, none of which re-read the clock.

Both clock boxes are date and time controls, and both round-trip to the minute — a punch silently truncated to midnight would collapse every attended figure to zero or to a whole number of days. Leave clock out blank for a shift that is still running: attended minutes are then genuinely unknown, and every figure derived from them answers "not yet" rather than zero. That is a deliberate design decision and not a gap in the data — a running shift reported as 0% utilised with infinite gap time would fill the exception queue with people who are simply still working. It can also be stamped later with the Clock out button on the shift's page, which records the moment the button was pressed; set it here instead when the punch was missed and is being reconstructed, because that is a different act with a different truth value.
Four rules are enforced on save and each protects a number that ends in a payroll export: the clock-out must be after the clock-in; a single session may not run longer than two days (long enough for an overnight shift closed out late the next morning, short enough that a mistyped year is a rejection rather than an overflow); a worker may have only one open shift, or the clock-out button would have to guess which one it is closing; and two shifts for one worker may not overlap, because double-counted minutes are double-counted payroll hours and nothing downstream can detect them — the export would simply report a longer day. A shift ending at 14:00 and one starting at 14:00 do not overlap: that is a handover, not a clash.

Notes and provenance

Provenance is stamped, never typed
{% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.notes %}
Notes belong to the shift and stay with it. A reason for withdrawing a shift is not a note — it is asked for by the Cancel button and lands in the audit trail, so it cannot overwrite what the shift itself says.

How this record arrived is not on this form, and must not be. The source, the login that filed it, the login that punched it and the close and approval stamps are all written by whichever code path actually files or moves the shift. A shift saved here is stamped supervisor entry with no punching login, because nobody punched — and that blank is the truth rather than missing data. Letting any of them be typed would let a hand-keyed shift claim to have come from a badge reader that never saw the worker, on a record whose minutes are read by the payroll export. Neither stamp is re-applied on an edit: correcting a typo on a shift the worker punched in themselves did not turn it into a supervisor entry.

Nothing derived is typeable either. Attended, booked, direct, indirect, gap, over-booked, earned minutes, performance, utilisation, units per hour and accuracy are all recomputed on read and have no column at all — so there is nothing here for a form to disagree with, and no way for a stored total to drift from the rows it was built from.

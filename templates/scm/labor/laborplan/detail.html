Hours per shift and productivity are not decoration — they are the two divisors every required headcount on this page went through: required minutes = standard minutes × 100 ÷ productivity, then headcount = required minutes ÷ (hours per shift × 60). At {{ obj.productivity_pct|floatformat:2 }}% a bucket costing 1,000 standard minutes needs {{ obj.productivity_pct|floatformat:2 }}% of a site's day rather than 100% of it, which is the honest number to roster against — planning to the standard and hoping is how a site is understaffed every day and nobody can point at the assumption that did it. Headcount is left fractional on purpose: 2.3 and 3.0 are different staffing conversations, and rounding at the line would hide six activities each needing 0.4 of a person sharing four bodies between them.

The history above is derived at generate time and never stored, so a regenerate always reflects what actually happened rather than a stale copy of it. Manufacturing draws, MRO spares, transfers and adjustments are deliberately excluded from the movement history: a transfer is posted as a pair across two locations and would double-count one physical touch, and a quantity changing on paper is not a quantity anybody carried.

This plan has no grid

A draft plan is the staffing question and nothing more — the generator is what builds the answer, and pressing Generate above is the only route into planned. There is deliberately no activity picker anywhere on this page: the generator emits one line per bucket per direct activity that has an active standard covering this site, so widening what a plan covers is a matter of adding a standard rather than maintaining a second list.

The grid is paginated by the database, not sliced after the fact — a plan may legitimately hold {{ max_plan_lines }} rows and fetching them all to draw fifty would be the payload rather than the page. That asymmetry is the model: letting a planner edit the required figure would let them close the gap by editing the question instead of by rostering somebody.

The ladder is draft → planned → approved → archived, and Generate is the only route into planned: the grid is the plan, so “the generator has run” and “this plan has lines” are the same fact and do not need two flags. Approving a plan with no lines is refused outright — it would approve nothing, and would put the green badge on the one plan in the workspace that has never been costed. Archive is the safe retirement and delete is not: an approved plan is the roster a site staffed to and an archived one is the record of a decision somebody made, so neither may be destroyed, while an archived plan stays readable forever. Nothing on this page posts a stock movement or drafts an accounting document — the payroll hand-off is a CSV export and lives nowhere near a foreign key.

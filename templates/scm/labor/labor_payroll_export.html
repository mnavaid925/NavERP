Sorted by worker name so one period's file diffs cleanly against the previous one. Every hour figure is to two decimals; a dash means the question has no answer for that worker in this window, and is never the same thing as 0.00 — which is why 0.00 is still printed wherever it is the true answer.

How each column is worked out

Work days
Distinct work dates with an approved shift. Two shifts on one day is one work day.
Attended
Clock-out minus clock-in, summed. Blank while no approved shift in the window has been clocked out.
Booked
Every minute booked to an activity on those shifts, direct and indirect together.
Direct
Value-adding work — picking, packing, put-away, counting, loading.
Indirect
Necessary time that moves no unit — travel, breaks, training, meetings, waiting, cleaning.
Unaccounted
Attended minus booked across the whole period, floored at zero. Time on the clock that nothing was booked against.
Over-booked
Booked minus attended across the whole period, floored at zero. Usually a forgotten stop or a double-scanned badge.
Earned
The engineered standard for the work actually done. Blank when nothing they did carried a standard.
Performance
Earned ÷ the direct time that carried a standard. Unmeasured minutes are excluded from BOTH sides, so work nobody has time-studied yet cannot drag somebody down.
Utilisation
Booked ÷ attended, over closed shifts only. A shift with no clock-out yet has no denominator, so it is left out rather than divided by nothing.

{{ gap_note }}

Every one of these is unanswerable rather than zero when its denominator is missing, and prints as a dash. That is not a display nicety: a 0% utilisation against a named person on a page a pay clerk reads is a claim about how they spent their shift, and this page will not make one it cannot support.

This page queries none of them, writes to none of them, and holds no foreign key into any of them — the hand-off deliberately stops at a file that a person carries across, because a second writer of one hours figure is exactly how two systems come to disagree about what somebody is owed.

In particular, nothing here drafts an accounting.PayrollRun. A payroll run is a whole-company period accrual — one document for the period, with no employee lines and no hours columns anywhere on it. There is no field on it for any figure in the table above, so raising one from warehouse labour would not be merely redundant, it would be wrong: it would state a company-wide accrual as though a subset of one department's shifts had produced it. The reconciliation between this file and hrm.AttendanceRecord happens on (worker, work date) by eye, in the payroll office, by somebody accountable for the result — never by a foreign key drawn between two systems that are counting different things.

Approval is the export lock. There is no "exported" flag and no exported_at column to set: a download is a GET, and a GET that wrote one would make a refresh, a browser prefetch or a crawler indistinguishable from a second export. "Who downloaded what, and when" is an audit-log question, and it is answered there.

The split between productive work and everything else is the line the whole module is built on — a performance figure is earned minutes over direct minutes, so an activity on the wrong side of it moves every number on the scorecard. The two halves are paired in both directions and the save is refused either way round: productive work records the units it completed and carries no reason; non-productive time carries a reason and completes no units. A reason with no required companion is a bucket nobody can total afterwards, which is the entire point of filing it.

Waiting for equipment, system down and waiting for work are the lost time the industry names separately — sanctioned, in the sense that nobody chose to spend it. The activity sizes the loss; the reason beside it is what a supervisor can actually act on.

Leave ended at blank while the work is still in progress — a running activity books no minutes until it is given an end, which is the honest state rather than a zero. The booked duration is derived from these two times and never typed, so the interval and its length can never disagree. A single booking is capped at 24 hours; a longer stretch is several activities, one per stretch of work. {% if not is_edit %}

Units are counted in the standard's own basis — units, lines, cases or pallets — which is what makes minutes per unit mean anything: 0.4 minutes is fast per pallet and absurd per case. Errors are the one recorded accuracy input; the accuracy percentage is derived from the pair and never stored, so correcting a mis-typed quantity corrects the percentage with it. Errors cannot exceed the units completed, or a worker who made three mistakes in two picks would read as −50% accurate.

Booked against

At most one task may be named, and the one that is named has to match the work — a pick task cannot carry cleaning minutes, or every board that reports "time booked against this task" reports a number that was never spent on it. Time spent on two tasks is two activities. The link is a pointer out to the warehouse task and adds nothing to it: 4.14 writes no column into 4.4's tables, posts no stock movement and drafts no accounting document.

The three dropdowns offer this workspace's still-open tasks, plus whichever task this activity already names even if it has since closed — a task legitimately finishes while its booking is still being corrected, and dropping it out of the list would silently null the pointer on the next save, keeping the minutes and losing the record of what they were spent on. They are deliberately not narrowed by the activity type above: that match is enforced when you save, where it also holds against a crafted request, and narrowing here would render three permanently empty selects on an unbound form. Reference is the honest fallback for work with no task record at all — it exists so nobody reaches for whichever pointer was nearest just to have filled something in.

The standard is not resolved again. The earned minutes are recomputed from the three determinants already snapshotted on this row and the quantity you type here, so fixing a mistyped figure fixes the measurement with it — while editing the standard in the library changes nothing on this row at all. Re-resolving would mean a correction made today silently re-measured work done months ago against a standard that has since been re-timed, which is the exact failure the snapshot exists to prevent.

The one honest edge: changing the activity type leaves the standard filed for the original type in place. Nothing pretends otherwise — you will be told so after saving. If the work really was a different job, delete this booking and file it again so it is measured against the right standard.

{% else %}

When this activity saves, the labor standard governing this kind of work, at {{ session.location.code }}, on the day it was actually done is resolved once and its three determinants — fixed minutes, minutes per unit and the allowance — are copied onto the row. Every performance figure this booking ever shows is computed from those copies. Editing or deleting the standard afterwards cannot move them, which is what lets a three-month-old figure be reproduced exactly as it was shown at the time.

If no active standard covers it, that is not an error: the booking is recorded as unmeasured, earned minutes and performance stay blank rather than reading as zero, and the row drops out of both sides of the shift ratio. Not every job in a warehouse has been time-studied, and printing 0% against {{ session.worker.name }} would render an unmeasured job as a failing one. Indirect time is unmeasured by design — break time is not earned time — so no standard is resolved for it at all.

{% endif %}
Neither the standard nor any of the snapshot figures is a field on this form, and none of them should ever become one: a standard a user can type is not a measurement, it is a claim — on a record that feeds a payroll export.

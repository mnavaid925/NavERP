The support case

Opened server-side, with this row

These two boxes are the only part of the case anybody types. Everything else about it is derived when the row is saved, by the one method allowed to create a case — which is what stops a console inventing a ticket type, and a portal user filing against a company that is not theirs. The case and the inquiry are written in a single transaction, so a submission this form refuses leaves no orphaned, customer-visible ticket sitting in the CRM queue.

What was reported, and what was asked for

Two questions, not one

The type is what went wrong; the requested resolution is what the customer wants done about it. They are recorded separately because "my box arrived damaged, just credit me" is a different job from the same damage with "send another one", and an agent who has to infer the ask guesses wrong. Both are closed sets on purpose: which failure mode drives the volume is then a total anyone can compute, rather than a pile of free text nobody can add up.

The supply-chain context

Pointers, not copies

This block is the entire reason the model exists — it is what a plain support ticket cannot say. Every one of these is a pointer at a document another sub-module owns: the order and the line belong to order management, the parcel to transportation, the invoice to receivables. Nothing here copies a figure out of them, so none of it can go stale, and nothing on this page edits them either.

PairingWhat the save refuses, and what it would otherwise let through
Line vs. order A line always has a parent, so a line that is not the named order's is rejected — and so is a line attached to an inquiry naming no order at all. Without that one comparison, a short-shipment claim can quote a quantity against another customer's line.
Parcel vs. order Checked only when an order is named: attaching a parcel that is not linked to it asserts "this shipment is part of that order" with nothing in the data behind the claim. An order-less inquiry — a quality question about goods already delivered — may still name the parcel.
Anything from another workspace Refused at the model boundary as well as narrowed in these dropdowns. A narrowed select is how the page helps; it has never been what holds against a crafted submission.
None of the four is narrowed to open or recent documents. An inquiry is very often filed precisely because the order closed, the parcel was delivered or the invoice went out — the subject being finished is the reason somebody is arguing about it.

How much is claimed

Needs a line

Fill this in for a short shipment or a damage claim, and leave it blank for everything else — blank means "the whole thing, or not a quantity question at all", which is a real answer rather than a gap. It is refused without a line, because a quantity has to be a quantity of something, and it is refused above what that line ordered: a claim covering more units than were sold is a credit note waiting to be written for goods that never existed.

How this console is worked out

Nothing here is stored. The allocated and backordered quantities are summed from the allocation ledger at read time, so they cannot drift from it the way a cached column would; the shipment status, its ETA and its last known location are read from 4.6's own records; and the tracking state, the ETA roll-up and the proof-of-delivery state are worked out on this page from those figures. No stock move is posted, no bill is drafted and no journal entry is written.

The tracking state is decided in one order — not shipped, then exception, then delayed, then delivered, then on the way — and the filter above is the same rule written in SQL. That is why a row the filter selected can never chip as something else. An order counts as delivered only when every live shipment on it is; a cancelled booking is left out of that roll-up, because a withdrawn booking is not a movement of goods, but it is still counted and shown so it is not quietly dropped.

Delayed means past the delivery date we planned for with the parcel still expected to move. It is our own arithmetic, not something a carrier reported — the exception column says which of the two you are looking at in plain words, and the words are the ones a customer can be given as they stand. A raw carrier code never reaches this page.

Proof of delivery is its own column because it answers a different question from "where is it". A parcel can be delivered and still have no signature against it, and that row — delivered, unproven — is the one an invoice dispute turns on.

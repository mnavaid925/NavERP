The limits

Signed — minus eighteen is an ordinary value here

{{ storage_condition_ranges_note }}

{% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.storage_condition %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.min_temperature %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.max_temperature %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.warning_margin_c %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.setpoint_temperature %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.humidity_min %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.humidity_max %}
A one-sided band is legitimate. Leave the lower limit blank for a chiller that can never be too cold, or the upper one for the reverse — blank means "no limit on that side" and is not read as a limit of zero. A reading exactly on a limit is in range, which is what "2 to 8 °C" means everywhere it is printed. An active monitor must carry at least one limit: without one nothing it reports can ever be in or out of range, every badge reads "not recorded" forever and the detector skips it silently. Park a point on Inactive or In Calibration while you work out what its band should be.
The warning margin is a magnitude rather than a temperature — it says how many degrees before a limit to start warning — so unlike the boxes around it, it cannot be negative. It must also stay under half the band width, or the two warning zones meet in the middle and the monitor warns while sitting exactly where it is supposed to be; a warning that is always on is a warning nobody looks at. The setpoint is a record of what the unit was told and not a control channel: nothing here commands a reefer, and the number exists so a unit drifting away from its instruction is visible. Humidity is recorded because half the surveyed loggers report it, but nothing detects on it this pass — a humidity breach is a second episode type with its own duration and grace rules.

What the detector reads

The grace period is the most important setting here
{% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.excursion_grace_minutes %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.logging_interval_minutes %}
Without a grace period every door-open is an incident, the queue fills with noise inside a week, and then nobody reads it — which is the actual failure mode a temperature-monitoring system dies of. A breach shorter than the grace is still recorded; it simply does not read as reportable. Editing this setting moves the answer for episodes already on file, and that is intended: there is no stored "reportable" flag to freeze last month's policy onto this month's incidents.
The logging interval does two jobs. It is snapshotted onto every reading as that row's weight, which is what makes mean kinetic temperature and time-out-of-range correct when the interval changes mid-window — a single hot hour must weigh one hour and not "one row". And it is what "still reporting" is measured against: a monitor reads as offline once its newest reading is older than three logging intervals, which tolerates one missed transmission plus a retry before crying wolf. Changing it here affects future rows only; last month's arithmetic keeps the weights it was filed with.

Calibration

Audit trail, not a compliance claim
{% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.calibrated_on %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.calibration_due_on %} {% include "partials/form_field.html" with field=form.calibration_reference %}
An uncalibrated sensor invalidates every record it produced, which is why this is the one genuinely persistent artefact on the row. Leaving all three blank is allowed and is reported as "no certificate recorded" — its own state, kept apart from "in date", because a data gap is not a due date that has not arrived. It is never shown green: not knowing whether a probe is trustworthy is not the same as knowing that it is. The certificate PDF attaches as an ordinary document; there is no separate attachment table here.
NavERP records these dates and audits every change to them. That is an audit trail and nothing more — it is not 21 CFR Part 11 or EU Annex 11 conformance: there is no electronic signature, no re-authentication at signing and no validated-system evidence. Over-claiming here would be worse than the gap.

Lifecycle

Only active monitors are detected against, so Inactive, In Calibration, Retired and Lost each take a point out of the sweep without deleting the deployment or its history. Lost is worth reaching for rather than folding into retired: a device nobody can find is watching nothing while every page still lists the thing it was meant to watch as monitored. Retiring is the move to reach for instead of deleting — a delete is refused outright once any excursion references this monitor, and where it does succeed it takes the entire append-only reading history with it.
The deployed date is enforced rather than decorative: a reading dated before it is refused, because on a re-usable logger such a row belongs to an earlier deployment and filing it here would attribute another consignment's journey to this one. Leave it blank if this monitor has no meaningful start date.

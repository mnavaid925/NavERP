# Research — SCM 4.10 Returns Management (Reverse Logistics)

Produced by a 13-agent workflow: three market-research lenses (retail returns platforms, ERP RMA,
reverse-logistics + warranty claims) and three codebase explorers (the stock side, the money side,
the public-portal side), then three independent design proposals, two judges and a completeness

## Discovery report 1

Money-side map for SCM 4.10. **There is no CreditNote model, no Refund model, and no store-credit/customer-balance concept anywhere in the repo.** `apps/accounting` has 28 concrete models; the ONLY customer-credit mechanism is a field: `Invoice.kind` ∈ {`invoice`, `credit_note`} on the ordinary AR `Invoice` (same table, same positive-total shape, same `INV-` numbering) — and `kind="credit_note"` is read in exactly ONE place in the whole codebase (the cash forecast excludes it from inflows). `invoice_post` posts Dr AR / Cr Income regardless of `kind`, so a credit note posted through the built button posts backwards. Two cross-module hand-off precedents exist and both draft a DRAFT accounting document and link it by a nullable FK, never a JournalEntry: SCM 4.6 `freightinvoice_handoff` → `accounting.Bill` (AP side) and CRM `dealinvoice_from_quote` → `accounting.Invoice` + `InvoiceLine` (AR side). `Payment` is direction-unified (`in`/`out`) but `payment_confirm` posts an `out` payment as Dr AP-liability / Cr Bank — i.e. a customer refund paid through the built Payment flow hits the AP account. Nothing in `apps/scm` imports `_post_journal_entry`, `JournalEntry` or `JournalLine`. `SalesOrder.invoice` is set only by a human POST in `salesorder_mark_invoiced`, never automatically.

### VERDICT — what "Refund Processing" CAN actually do today without violating L29
CAN (fully supported by existing code, zero new accounting tables, exact precedent):
(a) Draft a customer CREDIT NOTE — `Invoice.objects.create(tenant=..., party=<the customer Party>, kind="credit_note", status="draft", issue_date=..., currency=..., notes="...")` + one or more `InvoiceLine` rows + `inv.recalc_totals()`, all inside atomic(), then store it on a nullable `editable=False` FK on the 4.10 row and stop. This is literally the CRM dealinvoice_from_quote shape applied to AR, and structurally identical to freightinvoice_handoff. It is the only refund artifact accounting can represent.
(b) Draft a vendor `Bill` for a SUPPLIER warranty recovery — verbatim the freight hand-off, party = the supplier Party.
(c) Read-only reporting off `Invoice` / `Payment` / `CustomerProfile` (compute-then-convert).
CANNOT (would violate L29 or contradict built behaviour):
- Post any JournalEntry, or call `_post_journal_entry` (nothing in scm ever has).
- Create a `Payment` (direction="out") to actually pay the customer back: that is accounting's own draft→`payment_confirm` flow, and confirm posts an `out` payment as Dr AP-liability / Cr Bank, which is wrong for a customer refund — SCM must not reach into that.
- Issue/post the credit note. `invoice_post` is @tenant_admin_required in accounting and posts Dr AR / Cr Income irrespective of kind; 4.10 must leave the row `draft` and tell the user to finish it in Accounting (exactly what both precedents' success messages say).
MUST BE A DOCUMENTED STAND-IN:
1. "Store credit" — there is NO store-credit or customer-balance table anywhere. Either 4.10 records the intent as a status/enum on its own RMA row (a recorded decision with no financial object, the same posture as NonConformance.cost_of_quality) or it drafts a `kind="credit_note"` Invoice and documents that the credit note IS the store credit until a module owns customer credit balances. Do NOT invent a CustomerCredit table in scm.
2. The credit-note direction gap — an issued credit note today posts as a normal invoice and ADDS to the customer's open-exposure sum used by `_evaluate_hold`. Whatever 4.10 drafts must be documented as "draft only; accounting owns issuing", and 4.10 must not compensate by touching the ledger.
3. Refund METHOD (original tender / bank transfer / store credit) has no home in accounting until a Payment exists — record it as a choice field on the 4.10 row, not as an accounting object.

## Discovery report 3

NavERP has TWO distinct customer-facing surfaces already in production code, and 4.10's "Return Portal" must pick one of them (or both, as CRM 1.4 did).

(A) **Unauthenticated bearer-token pages.** Six exist (CRM case-status, KB article, KB vote, landing/web-to-lead, survey respond, event RSVP; CRM sign-document; HRM careers list/apply). The mechanism is identical everywhere: a `models.CharField(max_length=64, unique=True, editable=False, null/blank=True)` named `public_token` (or `token`), filled once in `save()` with `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)`, routed as `path("<thing>/<str:token>/", view)` with **NO decorator at all**, and resolved by `get_object_or_404(Model, public_token=token, <state guard>)`. There is no expiry on ANY token — the only expiry in the repo is `ContractDocument.expires_at`, checked in the view body, not on the token. There is no rate limiting anywhere (only `# WARNING: ... add per-IP rate-limiting (django-ratelimit)` comments). CSRF **is** enforced (global `CsrfViewMiddleware`, `{% csrf_token %}` in every public form; the only `csrf_exempt` in the repo is the Stripe webhook).

(B) **Login-gated portal-user pages.** CRM 1.4 `portal_case_*` — a real Django `User` bound to a `core.Party` through `CustomerPortalAccess` (`portal_user` OneToOne + `customer_party` FK + `can_submit_cases`), `@login_required`, then `_customer_portal_access(request)` returns the row or bounces to the dashboard.

**Tenant resolution for public views:** `TenantMiddleware` is NOT bypassed for public routes — it runs on every request and sets `request.tenant = None` for anonymous users. Public views therefore never read `request.tenant`; they take the tenant **from the resolved object** (`case.tenant`, `page.tenant`, `req.tenant`) or, where there is no object, from an explicit `?tenant=<slug>` query param (HRM careers list). `config/urls.py` mounts NO public/root routes — every public page lives under its owning app prefix (`/crm/sign/<token>/`, `/hrm/careers/<token>/apply/`).

**Print convention:** print pages are NOT `base.html` children. They are standalone `<!DOCTYPE html>` documents with an inline `<style>` block, a `.toolbar` div containing `<button onclick="window.print()">Print / Save PDF</button>` + a "Back to X" link, and `@media print { .toolbar { display: none; } body { padding: 0; } }`. Every one is `@login_required` and tenant-scoped; none is public. There is no shipping-label, packing-slip or BOL print page anywhere in SCM today.

**Bottom line for 4.10:** a Return Portal can safely be (1) an unauthenticated `rma_public(request, token)` page keyed on `RmaRequest.public_token` for status + label print, and/or (2) a re-use of the existing `CustomerPortalAccess` login. It CANNOT be a self-service "request a return" page for a stranger — nothing in the codebase lets an anonymous visitor prove they own an order; the closest precedents (careers apply, web-to-lead) both CREATE a new record rather than look one up. And lesson **L32** forbids pointing the NavERP sidebar bullet "Return Portal" at either gated page — it must point at a staff-facing list.

### The document/ownership boundary (the question asked)  _(bullet: all five)_
Every ERP surveyed splits a return into four documents with three owners. (1) AUTHORIZATION — SAP returns order, D365 return order (a sales order of type 'Returned order' whose RMA number is the alternate key), NetSuite Return Authorization, Infor RMA header+lines, Acumatica RM order. It owns NO stock and NO money: NetSuite states the RMA is a non-posting transaction, and D365 says the return order 'doesn't require that the company accept the returned products or provide a credit'. (2) RETURN RECEIPT — owns THE STOCK: SAP returns delivery + goods receipt into blocked returns stock, D365 arrival journal/registration (optionally a quarantine order), NetSuite Item Receipt, Infor RMA Return Transaction into a Disposition Warehouse, Acumatica return shipment with a 'Receipt' operation, Odoo reverse transfer. (3) DISPOSITION — owns what the stock becomes; in D365 it commits at packing-slip update. (4) CREDIT NOTE — owns THE MONEY, always a separate AR document raised against the original invoice. For NavERP: SCM owns (1)(2)(3) and posts StockMove; accounting owns (4). SCM drafts accounting.Invoice(kind='credit_note', status='draft') and stops — the 4.6 FreightInvoice->accounting.Bill precedent at apps/scm/models/TransportationManagement/FreightInvoices.py:63.

> **Evidence:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/sales-marketing/sales-returns ; https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1304530.html ; https://docs.infor.com/csi/9.01.x/en-us/csbiolh/lsm1454144038357.html ; https://help.acumatica.com/Wiki/Print.aspx?pageid=83c0e442-ce9e-4e57-8a52-b8b22f56fcbe ; https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/sales/sales/products_prices/returns.html ; https://www.all-for-one.pl/en/whitepapers/advanced-returns-management-in-sd-and-mm/

### CORRECTION to the existing file's scope — 'received but not yet decided' is a missing state  _(bullet: Disposition Management / RMA)_
The existing draft derives ReturnLine.quantity_received from ReturnDisposition rows, so a unit cannot be received-but-undecided. That is the most common real state: D365 routes an arrival line into a QUARANTINE ORDER and explicitly forbids setting a disposition code while the line is in quarantine; SAP holds goods in blocked returns stock and warns that follow-up activities will not execute until the inspection is confirmed. Two fixes: (a) add ReturnReceipt[RET-]+line as the stock-owning document and demote ReturnDisposition to its child (6 models — over the ≤5 budget), or (b) keep ReturnDisposition as the receiving row, add a 'received_pending' value, and make quantity_received = SUM of ALL rows regardless of disposition, with grading/disposition a second separately-stamped act on the same row. ### Disposition codes carry a FINANCIAL action as well as a logistics action  _(bullet: Disposition Management)_
D365 publishes the full built-in matrix and it is the most valuable published design in this domain — each code states its financial AND logistics implication: Credit only (no physical return; customer credited; scrap loss posted) / Credit (returned, back into inventory; credited) / Replace and credit (back into inventory; credited; separate replacement sales order) / Replace and scrap (returned and scrapped; credited; replacement order) / Scrap (returned/scrapped; credited) / Return to customer (sent back after inspection; NO credit except fees — used when the item was deliberately damaged or the warranty voided). SAP's equivalent is the logistical follow-up activity (to free available stock / to scrap / to supplier / to a specialist for repair) paired with a separate refund determination. The existing draft's disposition list (restock/refurbish/repair/scrap/return_to_vendor/donate/recycle/quarantine) is stock-only, missing credit_only and return_to_customer, and has no credit dimension. Add two executable columns per code: posts_stock (the 4.9 NonConformance.posts_stock precedent) and credits_customer.

### CORRECTION — the restock move's unit_cost will silently corrupt Item.average_cost  _(bullet: Disposition Management)_
D365 is explicit that a return line created via 'Find sales order' references the invoiced sales line so the item is valued at the same unit cost it was sold at, and it documents the failure mode when it isn't: using the item master price as the Return cost price produces a loss and an excessive credit. This bites NavERP hard — apps/scm/views/_helpers.py:130 _post_stock_move calls item.apply_receipt(quantity, unit_cost) on EVERY positive move, rolling the cached weighted average forward. A restock posted at the SALE PRICE (which is what ReturnLine.unit_price snapshots) would inflate average_cost for every later valuation. The restock StockMove.unit_cost must be a COST (the sold line's cost, or item.average_cost as the stand-in), never the price, and must be a separate field from unit_price on the line.

### How a RETURN receipt differs from a normal goods receipt (six real differences)  _(bullet: Disposition Management / RMA)_
1) Counterparty is a customer and the source doc is an RMA, not a PO. 2) It is routinely UNANNOUNCED/blind — D365 identifies the arriving package by RMA NUMBER on the Arrival overview and ships a whole 'sales returns unannounced' flow, so receive-by-RMA-number lookup is a first-class need, not a convenience. 3) Goods land in a returns/quarantine location (SAP blocked returns stock, Infor's dedicated Disposition Warehouse, D365's quarantine warehouse), not the normal receiving bin. 4) Cost comes from the original sale, not a purchase price. 5) Condition grading/defect coding is part of the receipt (SAP defect keys and inspection codes that can trigger the refund code; ReverseLogix SKU-level grades and reject codes). 6) Partial and split receipts are the norm — D365 splits the return line and mints a NEW LOT ID when the quantity does not share one disposition code, and Infor's extended dispositions put several codes with separate quantities on one line. Unlike a GRN there is no payable counterpart and therefore no 3-way match: the financial counterpart is a credit.

### VERDICT — WarrantyClaim vs NonConformance  _(bullet: Own a separate claim document; link to NCR by nullable FK; do not extend NCR)_

### Disposition decision tree — inventory-value effect per branch  _(bullet: Ten branches; exactly ONE re-adds inventory value)_
Restock = the only branch that puts value back on the balance sheet, re-added at a DECIDED unit cost (full cost for Grade A, written down otherwise). Refurbish/repackage = value re-added only on completion, at written-down cost + refurb cost. Repair-and-return-to-customer = ZERO inventory effect (it was never our stock); D365 lists this branch explicitly. Redeploy/transfer = value re-added then an ordinary internal transfer (ReverseLogix's 200-returned-valves example: no waste, no resale handling, no shipping beyond internal). Liquidate/secondary market = NOT sellable stock; what matters is realised recovery_value. Donate/recycle = recovery 0, no stock addition. Scrap = recovery 0, total loss on the unit. Return-to-vendor = our stock unaffected; the value chased is a CLAIM against the supplier, not inventory. Customer-keep / returnless refund = nothing moves (Optoro and Narvar take this at initiation when ship cost exceeds item value). Credit-only = D365's explicit disposition code: credit the customer with no physical return at all. KPIs every leader tracks and that fall straight out of this model: days-to-disposition, value-recovered-per-return, and % resold vs scrapped (ReverseLogix names all three).

### Grading and inspection of returned goods  _(bullet: The A/B/C/D ladder is the industry standard, with a known volume mix)_
Grade A — looks new, works, packaging intact → straight back to sellable stock (~60-70% of returns). Grade B — fully functional, minor cosmetic or packaging damage → refurbish or repackage, then resell (~15-20%). Grade C — functional but not full-price sellable → secondary channel: outlet, marketplace, palletised B2B liquidation (~10-15%); rarely sold unit-by-unit to consumers because of re-return risk. Grade D — not safely resellable or economically repairable → recycle/dispose (~5-10%).
